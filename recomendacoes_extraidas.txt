Escala da Topologia (52 nós, 17 fluxos):
Crítica esperada: Revisores podem argumentar que 52 nós é uma escala muito pequena para justificar claims de "Traffic Engineering" em redes de backbone.

Modelo de Tráfego (UDP vs. TCP):
Crítica esperada: O uso de injeção de taxa constante (estilo UDP) ignora a dinâmica de controle de congestão do TCP, que domina a Internet real. O TCP reage a perdas e atrasos, o que poderia alterar o comportamento das filas e a fronteira de viabilidade.
Como mitigar: Deixem claro na Seção IX que o foco é em fluxos com requisitos rígidos de QoS (como URLLC, vídeo ao vivo ou telemetria industrial), que frequentemente usam UDP ou protocolos semelhantes, e que a extensão para dinâmica TCP é um trabalho futuro.
Baseline de Aprendizado de Máquina (Q-learning Tabular):
Crítica esperada: Alguns revisores podem considerar o Q-learning tabular um baseline "fraco" ou desatualizado, sugerindo uma comparação com Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) moderno (ex: PPO, DQN com GNNs).

The continuous growth of Internet traffic turns capacity expansion into a recurring capital expense and motivates traffic engineering (TE) as a software-based alternative. Software-Defined Networking (SDN) exposes the routing layer to programmatic control, enabling QoS-aware path computation. This paper formulates the simultaneous routing of heterogeneous application flows as a constrained combinatorial optimization problem and solves it with a two-stage hybrid approach: an Ant Colony Optimization stage (AntNet) builds a pool of loop-free candidate routes, while a Genetic Algorithm (ACO+GA) or Differential Evolution (ACO+DE) selects the final route set.
Beyond the original scoring function, we introduce a revised formulation in which capacity and end-to-end delay are treated as hard constraints and load balancing is expressed as an explicit min–max link utilization objective. The revised model is solved exactly as a binary multi-commodity flow integer linear program (ILP). On a 52-node network and two synthetic 52-node topologies, across five load factors and twenty randomized replications, the exact ILP certifies that the pool-augmented hybrid attains the optimum of its search space in every replication. More importantly, it computes exact bottleneck lower bounds, proving, for example, that at 1.25× load on the sparse instance no routing can keep every link below 98.4% utilization, while shortest-path routing reaches 2.10× capacity and incurs 6.0 SLA violations against zero for the hybrid.
Packet-level simulation shows that the optimized network sustains URLLC-grade flows within a 150 ms delay budget up to 2× background amplification, whereas shortest-path routing violates the same budget at every tested load. A measured data-plane study further demonstrates that mapping the optimized paths to SRv6 segment lists reduces forwarding state by up to 4.1× (from 69 per-flow OpenFlow rules to 17 headend rules). The results position deterministic, constraint-explicit metaheuristics—with exact certification—as a practical and auditable traffic-engineering core for SDN controllers, and clearly delimit when they are preferable to learning-based routing.

Operators face a structural tension: traffic grows faster than revenue, and capacity expansion remains a recurring capital expense. Traffic engineering (TE) addresses this tension from the software side by routing existing traffic more intelligently rather than adding new links. Software-Defined Networking (SDN) provides the missing control point: by decoupling the control plane and exposing programmable forwarding, an SDN controller can compute and install paths that satisfy per-application QoS requirements instead of simply replicating shortest-path behavior.
This paper studies exactly that problem. Given a topology with per-link capacity and delay, and a set of concurrent applications with minimum bandwidth, maximum delay and priority requirements, the goal is to compute a route for every application that maximizes admission quality under QoS constraints. We make the following contributions, each responding to a concrete methodological gap in the heuristic SDN-TE literature:
Revised formulation. We analyze the scoring-function model commonly used in heuristic SDN-TE, identify its degeneracies, and reformulate the problem with hard capacity and delay constraints together with an explicit min–max utilization balancing objective.
Exact certification. We solve the revised model exactly as a binary multi-commodity flow ILP and the scoring model exactly over the candidate pool. The exact solutions certify that the pool-augmented hybrid attains the pool optimum in every replication and, more importantly, compute exact bottleneck lower bounds—proofs of the minimum achievable maximum link utilization.
Statistical evaluation at scale. We evaluate six routing methods on three topologies across five load factors and twenty randomized replications, with 95% confidence intervals and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests.
Measured SRv6 state reduction. We map the optimized paths to SRv6 segment lists and quantify the resulting data-plane state reduction relative to per-flow OpenFlow programming.
Slice isolation evidence. We connect the demand profile to 3GPP service classes and measure, in packet-level simulation, the stability of URLLC-grade flows under growing background load.
Learning-based baseline. We implement a tabular Q-learning routing agent under the same experimental protocol, grounding the comparison with learning-based approaches in measured numbers rather than assertions.

Conclusion (versão reescrita – mesmo estilo)
We reformulated QoS-aware traffic engineering for SDN by replacing a degenerate scoring function with hard capacity and delay constraints and an explicit min–max link-utilization objective. We then closed the methodological loop between heuristics and exactness: a two-stage hybrid (ACO candidate-pool generation followed by GA or DE selection) was certified against exact ILP solutions on three 52-node topologies, five load factors and twenty randomized replications.
The hybrid attains the certified optimum of its search space at nominal load, keeps SLA violations near zero in regimes that the exact model proves infeasible for unconstrained routing, and consistently outperforms both greedy baselines and a tabular Q-learning agent under stress. Mapping the optimized paths onto SRv6 segment lists reduces measured forwarding state by up to 4.1× and removes per-flow state from transit switches. At the same time, the constraint structure delivers measurable logical isolation of URLLC-grade flows under background amplification, without any physical network partitioning.
Taken together, the results establish deterministic, constraint-explicit metaheuristics—augmented by exact certification—as a practical and auditable traffic-engineering core for SDN controllers: exact where exactness is computationally affordable, and heuristic where scale demands it. The approach therefore offers a concrete alternative to purely learning-based routing whenever auditability, feasibility guarantees and predictable QoS isolation are required.
